Purpose and isolation from the original framework

The original evaluator intentionally used a simple invented deductible/cap formula. V6.0 adds richer synthetic insurance behavior without replacing that contract or invalidating its historical learning results. The advanced engine has separate effective-dated facts, independent calculations, APIs and database tables. It is not a real Medicare or commercial-payer rules engine. Explicit written benefit contracts

benefits_v60.json defines three half-open policy periods: January–June 2026, July–December 2026 and calendar 2027. Written BN-001 through BN-007 rules cover date/input validity, line eligibility, coordination of benefits, cost sharing, accumulators, ledger transitions and appeals. Existing teaching plans supply service/network/authorization/formulary facts. All extra cost-sharing values are invented.

Each line has a unique ID, service and bounded integer allowed amount. An optional other-payer payment cannot exceed the allowance. The remaining amount meets the embedded individual/family deductible, then a nominal copay, then payer coinsurance rounded down to cents. Actual member cost is constrained by individual/family out-of-pocket ceilings; plan payment is constrained by remaining annual benefit. Benefit-limit excess is noncovered and does not increase OOP. Nominal copay is an intermediate calculation, not a promise that this amount was charged after the OOP ceiling.

For a fresh 100,000-cent covered medical line, the first 2026 policy pays 71,200 cents; the second pays 70,800; the 2027 policy pays 68,000 after its OOP ceiling. These differ deliberately from the legacy evaluator's 90,000-cent result because they are different written contracts. Literal tests and runtime traces retain that distinction.

Stateful claims, family limits and money

benefit_claims.py stores multi-line results, partial denials, policy snapshots/hashes, revisions, events, yearly accumulators and a signed debit/credit ledger. F100 has three teaching members, making family limits meaningful even when an individual has not reached their own deductible/OOP ceiling. A separate family fixture is also available.

Adjudication reserves allocations. Finance can pay an adjudicated claim. An adjuster can replace lines on a paid claim: the old allocation is credited, replacement lines are calculated under the frozen original policy, and old/new payment entries are recorded atomically. Finance can reverse a paid claim, crediting its allocation and balancing the ledger. Owners can appeal; adjusters resolve by reprocessing corrected lines under the same policy. Appeals cannot invent coverage exceptions.

Every mutating request supplies an idempotency key, and subsequent operations require the current revision. The same key and identical actor/action/input returns its original result without another mutation; conflicting reuse is rejected. Concurrent operations serialize in SQLite transactions. Role and ownership checks apply before financial changes. Invalid replacement lines or storage errors roll back accumulator, ledger and claim changes together.

Reversal credits only the original claim allocation and never silently reprices later claims. This is an explicit teaching simplification. Policy snapshots also prevent a later edit to current facts from silently repricing a historical adjustment. An added regression test changes the current coinsurance fact and confirms that the old claim still uses its original policy.

Independent verification

benefit_reference.py reads authored facts independently and uses Fraction-based arithmetic with different control flow; it never calls production pricing. Literal examples cover policy boundaries, other-payer coordination, partial coverage, adjustments, reversals and annual reset. A seeded property test checks 500 generated combinations for money conservation, exact reference agreement and accumulator ceilings. Separate tests cover the third family member, invalid money types, ownership, appeal resolution and rollback.

The governing accounting identity is allowed amount = other-payer payment + member amount + plan payout + noncovered amount. It is checked alongside future-state accumulator changes; comparing payout alone would miss allocation defects.

Outcome and limits

The engineering hub supports a complete submit/pay/adjust/reverse journey through actual HTTP, with readable JSON totals, line outcomes, policy and rule traces, ledger entries and history. Legacy claim data is not migrated into this different engine. The final data audit proves original rows are preserved.

This milestone does not implement real allowed-amount negotiation, actual deductible regulations, external payments, clinical judgment, EDI/FHIR certification, clinical appeals review or HIPAA certification. New BN rules do not expand the old AI DSL automatically; explicit authored and property/browser checks cover the advanced engine.
