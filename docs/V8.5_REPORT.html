Healthcare Quality Lab V8.5 Engineering Report

What we built and why

V8.5 targets complete first-attempt AI tests within an explicit time budget. V8.4 eventually produced a correct known pilot through a resumed path, but that did not establish uninterrupted first-attempt reliability. Its pricing stage generated three identical quotes, its lifecycle prompt carried unnecessary policy facts, and its shared timing estimate mixed two stages with different costs.

The work is in /Users/erdalkilic/Documents/Healthcare Quality Lab, on codex/v85-first-attempt-generation. VS Code was not opened. Other projects and the unrelated V4 draft are outside this change. Claims, benefits, identities and payments remain synthetic; this is a quality-engineering showcase and research framework, not real Medicare/commercial-policy compliance certification.

This version changes structure, instructions, planning and measurement. It does not fill expected values, increase a model's score by editing its answers, retry until something passes or count a resumed result as an original pass. The known pilot and one fresh case passed on their first attempts. Fresh strict correctness was 1/3; the other two cases were blocked. The full recorded results and limits follow below.

Smaller output through safe structural bounds

The new healthcare_quality/lean_expectations.py protocol retains two model calls: pricing, followed by lifecycle. For a single claim with one submit and only pay/read/appeal thereafter, the financial/account snapshot is unchanged. The generation schema therefore allows exactly one quote. This directly prevents the three-identical-quotes output that made the V8.4 pricing response unnecessarily long.

The bound is not blindly applied to complex scenarios. Multiple claims can change shared family balances; a later read of an earlier claim can therefore need another snapshot. Adjustment, reversal and resolution also change financial/account state. These paths retain a safe upper bound based on requested observations. In stepwise mode only the requested observation needs a quote. Historical offline fixtures verify that existing cross-claim scenarios still fit their bounds.

The server determines only permissible output structure and IDs. The model still authors every monetary, account and lifecycle expected value. The bound does not invoke the pricing calculator or determine the correct payout.

Lifecycle quote references now have an enumeration containing only IDs actually produced by the pricing call. Undefined references fail before assembly. The parser never guesses a missing quote or selects a reference-correct amount. V8.4's exact unused-identical-alias normalization remains unchanged; differing money, worksheets, duplicate IDs or missing references remain rejected.

Separate prompts with only relevant facts

V8.4's pricing instructions inherited an introductory request to emit full observations and state worksheets, even though the stage's actual schema required pricing quotes. V8.5 replaces that mixed introduction with a dedicated pricing contract: quote fields, line arithmetic, rejected-line zero rules, remaining caps, used-account allocation and snapshot reuse.

The pricing prompt retains active policy intervals and governing amounts, plan service/network/authorization conditions, immutable inputs and family membership. It omits decorative plan descriptions and catalog deductible/limit fields that do not govern the benefit engine. Missing network, authorization and formulary flags are explicitly described as true by default, matching the implementation.

Lifecycle receives action metadata, operation keys, expected revisions and the model's own unverified quotes. It receives no raw policy table or line allowed amounts and no pricing formulas. Rules for reverse/adjust/appeal/resolve appear only when relevant. This reduces competing instructions without supplying correct answers. It still distinguishes zero-payout payment from no payment and preserves the ledger-entry/counter assertions.

Prompt-character comparison uses the exposed original pilot and identical one-quote model data for both versions. It measures logical prompt size, not tokenizer counts or a causal speed improvement. Generation schemas are additionally supplied by the provider. Actual token counts and timings are preserved separately with each live response.

Duration estimates and explicit operator budgets

V8.5's experiment_budget_v85.py estimates pricing and lifecycle separately, using same-protocol-version completed-response medians for matching stage/input shapes. Shape includes quote bound, input line count and observation count. When comparable samples are absent, clearly labeled conservative fallbacks apply: 130 seconds pricing and 90 seconds lifecycle for the installed 27B model, fifteen seconds per stage for smaller models.

Timeout observations are censored lower bounds rather than successful fast samples. Their elapsed/transport limits can raise an estimate and are disclosed in its details. Old protocol samples are excluded. Recorded model digests and hardware can still differ, and samples can be small; the estimate is planning information, not a completion guarantee or a capacity/SLA benchmark.

The browser has no preselected time budget. The API and CLI require an explicit 1–300 second value; absent or invalid values are rejected before generation. The UI shows the pricing/lifecycle breakdown, request count and whether the selection fits the chosen budget. Changing selections invalidates the estimate, including delayed stale responses. A user may deliberately choose less time than estimated, with an explicit interruption risk.

Admission-time budget semantics remain intact: model-availability preflight is separate and bounded, per-call timeouts are clipped to remaining job time, late answers are fenced, and the generation lock is held until the worker actually returns. Backend cancellation is best effort. This version does not claim to terminate all backend computation instantly.

A stricter first-attempt scorecard

The new expectation_scorecard_v85.py requires a complete independently eligible original answer and the exact planned sequence of pricing/lifecycle calls, each once per requested scope, with frozen responses and no reused output. Two planned stages can count as one first attempt. Stepwise mode requires the corresponding stage pair for every requested step. Optional self-review never replaces the original answer's score.

The scorecard separates original passes, wrong values, invalid output, provider errors, timeouts, interruptions, not-run cases, nonfirst/retried passes and resumed passes. Historical protocols contribute no V8.5 first-attempt denominator. An incomplete exercise has no final first-attempt percentage. A completed selected sample is still not broad model qualification.

Phase/shape metadata is now written before the provider call, not only after a successful response. This makes timed-out stages attributable. The generic lab change is optional and backward compatible; V8.4 and older routes, stores and scoring contracts remain available. V8.5 uses a separate store and readiness check.

Frozen, bounded actual evaluation

tools.evaluate_first_attempts_v85 prepares an input-only sealed plan with implementation/cohort hashes before any new inference. The primary model is the installed qwen3.5:27b: V8.4's smaller model failed all three selected cases, while the larger model supplied the eventual correct resumed values. This is a focused assessment of the improved larger-model path, not a comparison proving it universally superior.

The plan includes D83-01, the exposed original rejected-vision pilot, and H85-01/H85-02/H85-03: fresh numeric variants for rejected vision, hospital coverage and mixed medical/dental coverage. The variants are new input values, not independent new scenario families or a ten-input qualification set. Previously published H84 inputs are historical, not newly reserved.

Every case gets the same explicit 180-second admitted budget and at most one pricing/lifecycle pair. The entire exercise allows at most eight calls and a 600-second admission window. A full case budget plus preflight headroom must remain before another case begins. Timeout/provider error stops subsequent cases to avoid adding work behind an uncertain backend request. Unattempted planned cases remain visible. Model identity/settings or source drift invalidates pooled results.

There is no retry, resume, self-review, model swap, result-based prompt adjustment, numeric repair or hidden budget extension. Incorrect completed cases do not disappear from the sample. The exposed pilot is kept separate from the three fresh cases. Fresh first-attempt percentage is reported only if the fresh exercise completes.

Local full regression is run before inference so browser processes do not compete for memory during the model observations. Hosted CI runs independently. This scheduling choice is disclosed; different hardware load means these timings are not a controlled V8.4-versus-V8.5 latency experiment.

Actual results and what we achieved

The frozen exercise completed all four planned cases with eight actual local-model calls in 338.73 seconds overall. Every case retained its original 180-second budget. There were zero timeouts, retries, resumes, self-review calls or reference-assisted corrections. Each pricing response contained one quote, each lifecycle response referenced that quote, and no unused-alias normalization removed anything.

The original exposed pilot passed on its first complete attempt, with pricing taking approximately 47.07 seconds and lifecycle 34.79 seconds. It no longer needed the V8.4 lifecycle continuation. The fresh result is 1/3 strict first-attempt passes (33.33%), with all three fresh variants assessed. The exposed pilot is not included in that percentage. Both successful cases were rejected-coverage, zero-payout scenarios. The two nonzero cost-sharing cases failed. Thus first-attempt completion is demonstrated on this small exercise, while general expected-value correctness remains inadequate. Three numeric variants do not establish broad reliability, an 80% target or ten-input model qualification.

Hospital case H85-02 incorrectly used the whole 76,391-cent eligible amount as nominal deductible instead of the 10,000-cent policy-bounded amount. It predicted a 46,391-cent payout rather than 52,312 cents and propagated that wrong pricing into payment/ledger expectations. Mixed case H85-03 similarly used 54,731 cents as nominal deductible rather than 10,000, predicted payout 24,731 rather than 34,984, and gave nonzero pre-OOP/payer worksheet amounts to the rejected dental line. Full field differences are retained in trial-analysis.json. These were numeric/semantic failures, not missing quote IDs, malformed output or interrupted jobs.

The model's two eligible cases passed the normal public V8.5 export gate and actual HTTP execution, with every expected value unchanged. Execution was blocked while proposals were unapproved, then passed after explicitly scripted engineering inspection. This was not independent human review. Each case used isolated synthetic state. The two incorrect cases returned 409 EXPECTATIONS_BLOCKED and were not executed. Original model artifacts remained byte-for-byte unchanged throughout that verification.

The useful result is a complete first-attempt path for the known pilot and one fresh rejected-coverage case, plus honest rejection of remaining arithmetic failures. There was no post-result prompt tuning, numerical repair, extra model request or replacement of a failed case with a passing one.
